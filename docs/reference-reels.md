### What it does differently from our Diwali Lakshmi reel

1. **Many fast cuts:** roughly 1–2 s per shot (about 15–20 shots), against our 10 shots of ~3 s.
2. **Human reaction shots:** ordinary people's emotional faces (joy, folded hands) between the
   epic shots. Viewers see themselves in them. Ours had one villager, seen from the side.
3. **One "wow" centrepiece:** a flying palace, something no film budget would give a
   regional viewer. Comments: "Better than all Ramayan movies", "We want movie",
   "How to make such artwork?".
4. **Movement everywhere:** camera moves, motion blur and light flares between shots; nothing
   is static for long.
5. **Almost no on-screen text:** the story lives in a long English caption; the video ends on
   one gold title word for recall.
6. **Broad audience:** a pan-India legend told in English. Ours is Telugu-first (smaller,
   but less competition).
7. **The AI label didn't hurt:** 71K likes with "AI content" shown.
8. **Shares are ~10 % of likes:** festival content that people forward as a greeting.

### What to try in our next devotional Short

- 15–20 shots of 1–2 s, cut to the beat of the music; keep one 3–4 s hero shot.
- Add 3–4 reaction close-ups of adult devotees (joy, folded hands, lamp light on faces).
  No children (Flow rejects them; our rule).
- Plan one spectacle shot per story (a flying chariot, a giant deity in the sky, a temple
  rising from the sea) and put a glimpse of it in the first second.
- End on a gold title card (story name + "UmaAI") instead of a plain fade.
- Credits: more shots, but each clip is cut to 1–2 s, so one 8 s Veo clip can give 2–3 shots
  (different moments of the same clip): plan ~12 clips ≈ 120–180 credits.
